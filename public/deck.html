Bound

A surety bond for AI agents. If the guarantee is a lie, anyone can prove it — and the liar's own money is already where the proof can reach it.

01 / 08
The problem

“Trust me,
my agent is safe.”

Agents are handed spending authority faster than anyone can price the risk of giving it to them. That sentence is the current state of the art, and a counterparty cannot check it, size it, or recover against it.

Insurance for software agents does not exist because there is no shared, verifiable object to insure.

02 / 08
How it works

Three parties, one object

Operator
Publishes a certificate naming a bound — the most it will cover — and funds a reserve against it.
Auditor
Stakes their own slashable capital attesting the bound is honest. Earns premiums priced bound × duration.
Challenger
Proves fraud and is paid from the liar's money, which is already locked where the proof can reach it.

Three of the four fraud proofs are arithmetic on on-chain state. No referee — the contract reads the vault and does the subtraction itself.

What we built here
The gap

The operator was
printing its own
collateral

On the original deployment the operator issues the very token its reserves are denominated in. It funds a promise by minting what it promises. That is circular — and it is the whole reason a bond needs a bank.

05 / 08
The fiat boundary
TRY → USDC → reserve

A bond funded from a bank

A second deployment
The same seven contracts, denominated in money we cannot mint — the anchor's own USDC. Funded by a completed SEP-24 deposit.
A Turkish lira ramp
SEP-6 and SEP-38, because the ramps this market will have host no form — and SEP-24 cannot reach them.

Nothing is compiled in: the anchor's own stellar.toml decides the endpoints and the protocol. On that instance fundsReserve: true — a deposit funds a certificate's reserve instead of stopping in a wallet.
