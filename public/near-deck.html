/// The problem

An agent with a key
is an unpriced risk

Services are asked to sell to agents, and to extend them credit, on “trust me, my agent is safe.” Nobody can check that sentence, size it, or recover against it.

A prompt-injected agent spends whatever its key allows, and when harm happens there is no money set aside and no rule for who is paid first.

02 / 10

/// The solution

One certificate, three numbers you can check

Coverage = reserve + auditor stake. The bound is a meter, not a guarantee.

  • Reserve

    Pre-funded by the operator and locked in the contract. Arbitrated victims are paid from it first.

  • Slashable stake

    An auditor locks its own capital behind the certificate and is slashed for what the reserve could not cover.

  • Spend meter

    Every payment is limited by the float and metered in spent, and goes only to allowlisted recipients when the operator sets an allowlist.

03 / 10

/// How it works

Publish, confine, pay, claim, settle

WhoDoes
Operatorpublishes the certificate (bound, reserve, float cap, allowlist, expiry) and funds it
Agentaccepts once, then keeps only a function-call key that can call pay
Auditorattests by locking stake, earns the premium (less a 10% protocol fee)
Victimfiles a harm claim, which opens a claim window and freezes payments; the arbiter rules; when the window ends anyone closes it

Settlement waterfall, run mwfnc6q8: 120 = 100 reserve + 20 slash. The auditor's other 180 was not touched.

04 / 10

/// Why NEAR

The account model does half the enforcement

  • Function-call access key

    The agent's only key is scoped to the Bound contract and the single method pay, gas-only allowance.

  • Named accounts

    Certificates and allowlists name readable accounts, not key hashes.

  • View calls

    verify, get_cert and the access-key list are free reads anyone can make. No wallet, no indexer.

  • No code on the agent

    No contract and no global contract on the agent account: nothing on it can route around the meter.
